What is the Ramadan Forum?

The Ramadan Forum is an institutional event in Jeddah that convened media and diplomatic actors around the Muslim World League’s message and initiatives. Per published coverage, it took place on Wednesday 25 February 2026 CE, corresponding to 8 Ramadan 1447 AH. A Ramadan calendar imposes a different rhythm than sales exhibitions: shorter tour windows, higher protocol density, and an expectation of instant clarity before multinational news agencies.

In that setting, a pretty logo on a screen is not enough. The exhibition must explain documents and initiatives to guests who move for only a few minutes before formal speeches — which is exactly why a smart-maquette and interactive display system is an operating tool, not ornament.

Organizers: UNA and MWL Corporate Communications

The Forum was organized by the Union of OIC News Agencies (UNA), in cooperation with the Muslim World League’s Assistant Secretariat for Corporate Communications. That structure clarifies the audience: agency directors, journalists, and media officials — not homebuyers. Display language must be institutional, quotable, and cross-cultural without trivializing the content.

When the media partner is a union of news agencies, the exhibition becomes a briefing room before the session: a clear path, locked talking points, and content that does not contradict the official platform discourse.

Occasion: second anniversary of Building Bridges

The Forum marked the second anniversary of the document “Building Bridges Between Islamic Schools of Thought.” The document is a scholarly–approximate framework that needs fast visual explanation before an international media audience: what is the idea? who supervised it? how does it sit inside MWL’s initiative system? Without a spatial explanation layer, the anniversary collapses into a headline only.

H.E. the Secretary General praised the Forum and described the document as a unique scholarly encyclopedia supervised by senior Islamic scholars — wording that obliges the display system to seriousness and clarity, not cinematic excess.

Official and media attendance — as reported

Per published coverage (GrandLB and aligned sources such as Alnilin / AMI / Almajd), attendees included H.E. Secretary General Sheikh Dr. Mohammad bin Abdulkarim Al-Issa; OIC Assistant Secretary-General for Palestine and Jerusalem Affairs Samir Bakr Diab; Chechen minister Ahmed Dudayev; former Egyptian Minister of Awqaf Dr. Mohammad Mukhtar Jumaa; directors of 32 news agencies; and media/officials from 19 countries (Europe, South America, Australia, Canada, Africa). We do not invent attendance figures beyond the sources.

That mix imposes on the display system: instant multilingual readability, a protocol path that does not choke, and interaction points that do not require long technical coaching from the host.

Before speeches: the joint exhibition — where visual delivery matters

Before formal speeches, a joint exhibition for the Muslim World League and UNA opened, introducing initiatives. That moment — not the podium alone — is where the display system is tested under camera and guest pressure. Visitors pass a circular interactive maquette, visual presentations of MWL/UNA activities, and a compressed briefing path before seating for the session.

Graphics House designed and executed the exhibition / interactive smart-maquette visual system for the MWL side of this institutional showcase — a ProjectLaunch™–style delivery: lock the message, pack the assets, readiness for install, and visual consistency from first touchpoint to final screen. Full documentation: Makkah Charter — smart maquette & interactive environment.

Book launch and visual presentations of activities

The Forum included the launch of the book “Makkah Charter — A Message of Moderation and Tolerance…,” alongside visual presentations of MWL and UNA activities. The book locks the written narrative; the exhibition locks the spatial narrative. Both work when identity and message align — the role of branding & install and maquettes inside one package.

Visual presentations before agency directors are not a promo reel; they are a quotable briefing. Scenes should rest on initiative facts and clear geographic pathways — not effects that weaken credibility.

Why ProjectLaunch fits institutional events in Jeddah

ProjectLaunch™ in an institutional context means: one message, locked assets (maquette / interactive / environment), acceptance rehearsal before first-guest day, and a failure scenario. Unlike off-plan residential launch, the “product” here is documents, initiatives, and values — not units. Jeddah as a Gulf/international host city imposes real-hall lighting tests, install logistics, and transport — not studio-only sign-off.

Core lesson: explain the document to international media in minutes

A successful institutional exhibition explains documents and initiatives to agencies from 19 countries in a short tour window. The order that works: occasion context → spatial symbol (maquette) → data/initiatives layer → invitation to interact or capture a protocol photo. Reversing that order confuses non-specialist guests and wastes precious minutes before speeches.

Also: separate leadership imagery from initiative explanation. Imagery builds trust; the maquette builds understanding. Mixing both on one screen weakens both under a news-agency lens.

Acceptance checklist — VIP walkthrough for an institutional exhibition

  1. Message and values copy signed off by the owning institution before final production
  2. Content map frozen (documents / initiatives / languages) with a review date
  3. 3–7 minute VIP tour script plus a longer technical/media-delegation version
  4. Maquette interaction tested under real hall lighting and expected crowd density
  5. Non-interactive fallback when touch or network fails
  6. Identity unified across maquette, screens, boards, and displayed book
  7. Hosts trained on talking points — not only button-pressing
  8. Protocol path from entry to maquette point without choke points
  9. Protocol review of sensitive imagery before media broadcast
  10. Safe tear-down, packing, and transport plan for sensitive parts after the event
  11. Post-event link of assets to the internal delivery case study

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Treating an institutional exhibition like a residential sales gallery with the same still templates
  • Delaying maquette rehearsal until “opening day” before the Secretary General and agencies
  • Data screens without narrative: numbers without story confuse news directors
  • Hall lighting that washes out projection or touch without prior testing
  • Claiming news authorship or inventing attendance figures not in sources
  • Neglecting multilingual versions for guests from Europe, South America, and Africa

Practical steps for institutional and media teams

If you are planning an institutional exhibition or forum in Jeddah or the Kingdom: start with a message/document brief, then choose an asset pack (smart maquette + interactive + environment), then book an acceptance rehearsal before protocol. Use ProjectLaunch™ as the readiness frame; maquettes, interactive experiences, and branding as delivery services; and tie spatial planning to Jeddah.
